# Working papers
* _LongFinBERT: A Language Model for Very Long Financial Documents_, 2025, jointly with Erik-Jan Senn [[Paper]()]

  **Abstract:** This paper introduces LongFinBERT, a language model designed for excessively long financial documents. LongFinBERT demonstrates significant efficiency, requiring considerably less computational time and memory than other state-of-the-art language models. We apply LongFinBERT to two financial applications: (i) detecting financial misreporting, and (ii) examining stock market reactions to year-over-year modifications in firm disclosures. In misreporting detection, LongFinBERT outperforms accounting variables and other text-based models. In the second application, we find that investors respond to modifications in firm disclosures, as measured by LongFinBERT, with significantly stronger reactions during periods of high economic uncertainty. Importantly, traditional text-analysis approaches fail to capture this insight.

* _The power of firm disclosures in macroeconomic forecasting: A textual analysis approach_, 2025

  **Abstract:** This paper examines the predictive power of firm disclosures in macroeconomic forecasting. Leveraging advanced language models, I analyze the prevalence and tonal polarities of 31 accounting topics across 890,277 10-K and 10-Q filings. Compared to FRED-MD, a standard dataset in forecasting literature, and text-based features from news media, firm disclosures demonstrate superior performance in long-horizon forecasts, particularly for gross domestic private investment. The forecasting strength of firm disclosures is primarily driven by discussions on corporate income tax, with other topics like revenue performance and mergers and acquisitions also proving significant in specific contexts. Importantly, these predictive insights align closely with established economic theory. Finally, firm disclosures exhibit strong GDP and Investment forecasts under economic depression, compared to FRED-MD.

    **Presented in:**
  * Economic Lunch Research Seminar, Faculty of Business and Economics, University of Basel, April 2025, Basel, Switzerland
