# Working papers
* [Job Market Paper] _The power of firm disclosures in macroeconomic forecasting: A textual analysis approach_, 2025 

  **Abstract:** This paper examines the predictive power of firm disclosures in macroeconomic forecasting. Leveraging advanced language models, I analyze the prevalence and tonal polarities of 31 accounting topics across 890,277 10-K and 10-Q filings. Firm disclosures exhibit superior performance in long-horizon forecasts, with their predictive strength primarily driven by discussions on corporate income taxes, revenue performance, mergers and acquisitions, and repurchase agreement activities. These topics transmit information to macroeconomic outcomes through firms’ capital expenditures and dividend policies. Moreover, firm disclosures provide particularly strong forecasts of GDP and Consumption during periods of economic downturn, outperforming the FRED-MD dataset.

* _Read Them All at Once - Efficient Language Modeling for Very Long Financial Documents_, 2025, jointly with [Erik-Jan Senn](https://eriksenn.github.io/) [[Paper](http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5011898)]

  **Abstract:** This paper introduces LongFinBERT, a language model designed for excessively long financial documents. LongFinBERT demonstrates significant efficiency, requiring considerably less computational time and memory than other state-of-the-art language models. We apply LongFinBERT to two financial applications: (i) detecting financial misreporting, and (ii) examining stock market reactions to year-over-year modifications in firm disclosures. In misreporting detection, LongFinBERT outperforms accounting variables and other text-based models. In the second application, we find that investors respond to modifications in firm disclosures, as measured by LongFinBERT, with significantly stronger reactions during periods of high economic uncertainty. Importantly, traditional text-analysis approaches fail to capture this insight.
